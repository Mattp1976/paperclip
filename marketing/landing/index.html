§ I · What it does

Four moves, performed in order, every time.

Each outcome you ask for moves through the same four-stage cycle. Orqestra is the conductor; the agents are the orchestra. Both are doing the work — the conductor's job is to know when each instrument should sound.

  1. i.

    Plans the outcome from one sentence.

    Tell Orqestra what you want — "an intelligence report on agentic AI", "the September close" — and it returns a costed plan with the steps, the agents, and the budget before any work begins.

  2. ii.

    Routes work to the right agent.

    A workforce of specialists — research, finance, legal, ops, sales — each with their own instructions and tools. Orqestra hands every step to the one whose job it is.

  3. iii.

    Reviews itself before shipping.

    A reviewer agent reads what each operator produced, flags weaknesses, and asks for revisions. The output you see is the version that already survived a critique.

  4. iv.

    Tells you what it spent and why.

    Every run is logged with token cost, time, and the human equivalent of what was just done. No mystery bill at the end of the month.

§ II · How it works

The conductor, the score, the orchestra.

Orqestra is built around a deliberate pattern. A planning service writes the score. A router decides who plays each part. The orchestra of agents executes in parallel where it can, in sequence where it must. A reviewer listens before the cue is sent to the audience. Every motion is observable, costed, and auditable.

  1. Stage 01 · Intake

    You write one sentence.

    The outcome lands at /start. You name what you want, the audience, the deadline. No fields, no tickbox forms.

  2. Stage 02 · Planning

    The planner writes the score.

    An LLM reads your fleet of agents and your outcome, returns a step-by-step plan with named owners and an estimated cost. You see it before any token is spent.

  3. Stage 03 · Approval

    You approve, or revise.

    One click to start. Or edit the plan in place — swap the agent on a step, drop a step, raise the budget.

  4. Stage 04 · Routing

    Each step finds its specialist.

    The router picks the agent best suited to the step — based on role, capabilities, and historical performance.

  5. Stage 05 · Execution

    The orchestra plays.

    Independent steps run in parallel. Each agent has its own working folder, its own session, its own tools.

  6. Stage 06 · Review

    A reviewer critiques the output.

    A separate agent reads the work as a sceptical reader. Calls weaknesses. Asks for revisions. The version you receive has already survived this gate.

  7. Stage 07 · Assembly

    One coherent deliverable.

    An assembler agent stitches the parts together — your report, your plan, your model — into a single document with the right tone, structure, and citations.

  8. Stage 08 · Standup

    You get a daily summary.

    Every morning, a digest of what shipped, what's running, what stalled, and what it cost. Standup as email.

§ IV · Getting started

From sign-up to first finished outcome in three minutes.

No installation. No configuration. The first run pays its way in less than a coffee.

  1. Step 01 · 60 sec

    Sign up.

    Email and password at orqestra.run. No credit card. No verification email purgatory.

    You arrive directly at /start — a single prompt asking what you want done. There is no onboarding wizard.

  2. Step 02 · 30 sec

    Install a template.

    Pick a workforce: The Agent Collective (113 agents, full-service firm), Solo Consultant (3 agents, lean), or Engineering Squad (12 agents, builders).

    Templates are forkable. Edit any agent's instructions, add your own, remove the ones you don't need. Templates are also shareable — your fork can be installed by anyone you give the link to.

  3. Step 03 · 10 sec

    Describe an outcome.

    One sentence. "Produce a competitive teardown of Notion vs Coda." Or "Close the September books." Or "Find me 20 leads in fintech, London, Series B."

    Orqestra returns a costed plan in 5–15 seconds. You see every step, every owner, every estimated dollar before any work begins.

  4. Step 04 · 5 sec

    Approve the plan.

    One click. Or edit the plan in place — swap an agent, raise a budget, drop a step.

    Decisions are reversible. You can pause a run mid-flight, intervene with feedback, and resume.

  5. Step 05 · ~minutes

    Receive the work.

    Watch live, or close the laptop. The outcome appears in your inbox with the deliverable, the cost, the time, and the human equivalent.

    Every run produces an audit log: which agent did what, what was reviewed, how much each step cost, and which sources were consulted.

§ VI · Requirements

What you need.

Cloud at orqestra.run needs nothing but a browser. Self-host needs a Postgres and a place for Node 22 to run.

To use the cloud

Browser
Latest Chrome, Safari, Firefox, or Edge. No mobile app — yet.
Account
Email and password. No verification step. No credit card.
Anthropic API key
Optional, for direct billing. Without it, runs use the managed plan once it ships. console.anthropic.com
Network
Outbound HTTPS only. No ports to open, no IPs to allowlist.

To self-host

Runtime
Node 22.x, pnpm 9.x. macOS or Linux. Windows via WSL.
Database
Postgres 14+ (managed or local). An embedded Postgres is bundled for dev.
Storage
~2 GB for the binary and skills. Run logs grow with use; rotate weekly.
Secrets
ANTHROPIC_API_KEY required. Optional: OPENROUTER_API_KEY for fallback. Better-Auth secret for sessions.
Domain (production)
An HTTPS domain with valid certificate. Railway, Fly, or your own infra.

§ IX · Frequently asked

The questions we keep being asked.

Is this just a wrapper around Claude?

No. Claude (and other models, via OpenRouter) is the engine that powers individual agents. Orqestra is the orchestration on top: the planner, router, reviewer, assembler, the workforce of named specialists, the cost telemetry, the templates, the workspace.

You can swap the underlying model without changing how the rest of the system behaves. The orchestration is the product.

What happens if an agent fails?

Failures are first-class. Every run is logged with status, exit code, and stderr. A failed step does not silently roll the outcome forward — it appears as a failed step in the plan, with the error visible.

You can retry the step, edit its prompt, or reassign it to a different agent. The rest of the plan continues running where dependencies allow.

Can I self-host it?

Yes. The full repo is open and Dockerised. Postgres and Node 22 are the only hard requirements. Production needs a domain with HTTPS — Railway, Fly, Render, or your own infra all work.

See the README.md for a one-command setup, or the deployment doc for production hardening.

What about my data?

On the cloud at orqestra.run, your outcomes, plans, and run logs live in our managed Postgres. We do not train any model on your inputs or outputs. We do not share data between accounts.

Self-hosters keep everything on their own infrastructure. Bringing your own Anthropic key means model calls go directly to Anthropic without passing through us.

How is this different from LangChain, AutoGen, CrewAI?

Those are libraries. They give you primitives to build agentic applications. They do not give you a running company — a workforce, a UI, a planner, a budget, an inbox, a CEO who chases stuck work, a CFO who flags overspend.

Orqestra is opinionated where they are flexible. You install a fleet and start asking for outcomes the same afternoon. You can still extend with the SDK if you need to, but the default path is "use the workforce that ships with it."

Does it work for regulated industries?

The audit log is designed for it: every action, every model call, every cost, with timestamps and actor identity. Self-host puts the data fully in your custody.

SOC 2 and ISO 27001 are on the roadmap for the managed tier. Until those land, regulated buyers should self-host.

Is there an API?

Yes. Every action you can take in the UI has an HTTP endpoint behind it. Outcomes can be created via POST /api/companies/:id/outcomes, runs polled via GET /api/heartbeat-runs/:runId, agents managed via PATCH /api/agents/:id.

Webhooks for run completion are coming in the next release.
